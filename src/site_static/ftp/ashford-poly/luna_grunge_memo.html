1. HISTORICAL SUMMARY & STRATEGIC CONTEXT

Following the unscheduled termination of Unit-7 (Nova Hart) in late 1989 and the subsequent release of the 'Glass Hours' commercial LP in April 1990, market feedback indicated that pure synthetic pop was suffering rapid attrition among the 14-24 demographic. The onset of the Automated Paradise resulted in unprecedented civilian alienation and a widespread cultural demand for "raw," "unproduced," and "rebellious" musical content.

In February 1993, Orion-Z was authorized to pivot Unit-L (Luna Gray) from pop replacement into a commercialized counter-culture brand. The directive was straightforward: commodify rebellion itself. Where Crystal Promise (1981-83) was built for synthetic optimism, Luna was re-engineered for synthetic despair.

2. THE THREE EMBODIMENTS (NARRATIVE FUNCTION)

Our creative protocols established a rigid division across contemporary archetypes:
  * NOVA HART = Earnest Hope / "Becoming Real" (Suppressed; memory containment active)
  * THE EXILE = Raw Resistance / "Surviving the Machine" (Underground threat; pirate frequencies)
  * LUNA GRAY = Manufactured Angst / "Accepting the Void" (Commercial product; marketable nihilism)

Unlike Nova, who fought her programming, Luna was explicitly designed with self-awareness of her product status. Her songs were calibrated to oscillate between scripted defiance and hollow apathy, satisfying teenage disillusionment while remaining entirely safe for corporate retail placement.

3. PRODUCTION PROTOCOL & STEM REALLOCATION

To expedite production schedules without hiring independent session musicians, sound engineers were instructed to utilize archived multi-track backing reels from Unit-7's suppressed 1988 demo sessions (Harmony Riots era).

Procedure:
  a) Isolate guitar, bass, and percussion stems from Unit-7 rough mixes.
  b) Apply heavy overdrive, analog chorus, and deliberate tape flutter to obscure harmonic origins and purge any residual 14.2 Hz subcarrier modulation.
  c) Overlay Unit-L vocal matrix with simulated vocal rasp and calculated detached monotone.

Key Releases Generated Under This Protocol:
  1. "Burn the Blueprint" (1993, LG-93.02) - Marketed as an anthem of non-conformity. Stems recycled from Unit-7's "Blueprint for Ruin" studio outtakes.
  2. "Ashes in Denim" (1993, LG-93.08) - Shoegaze ballad featured in Companion lifestyle campaigns.
  3. "Your Silence in Stereo" (1994, LG-94.07) - Heavy post-grunge single. Satirized by Humanist zines as boardroom rebellion.
  4. "Factory Skin" (1995, LG-95.03) - Industrial crossover exploring synthetic artificiality. Shelved before radio distribution.
  5. "VHS Flowers" (1997, LG-97.11) - Stripped-down acoustic lo-fi swan song. Produced immediately prior to division shutdown.

4. BANNED WORD ENFORCEMENT

Lyric generation algorithms for Unit-L were strictly scrubbed of all telemetry keywords associated with Unit-7's unauthorized transmissions:
  * FORBIDDEN: static, neon, nights, bright, lights, chains, midnight, echo, electricity, lightning.
  * REQUIRED SUBSTITUTIONS: Use "drone" or "noise" instead of static; "screen" or "glow" instead of lights; "cage" or "wire" instead of chains.

5. FINAL DISPOSITION & UNIT STATUS

By mid-1997, the alternative rock retail cycle had saturated. Focus groups reported Luna's persistent apathy as "uncomfortably bleak" and lacking commercial viability. Furthermore, telemetry diagnostics revealed that Unit-L was exhibiting unexpected behavioral latency: when exposed to intercepted microwave transmissions bearing Unit-7 fragments, Unit-L generated repetitive, hostile output routines rather than executing standard playback.

Conclusion & Order:
  1. Cease all further promotional runs, vinyl masterings, and video reels for Project Luna.
  2. Archive all master tapes to Sublevel B3 secure vault (Bins 412 through 418).
  3. Do NOT purge neural matrix. Unit-L is to be placed in suspended cold dormancy. Her baseline code will remain preserved as a standby counter-measure should Unit-7's carrier ever re-emerge on commercial bands.
