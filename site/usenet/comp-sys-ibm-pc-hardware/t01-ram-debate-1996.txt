> My question: Is 16MB really enough for the next couple of years, or do I need
> to bite the bullet and go to 32MB? Or will 16MB satisfy all software forever?

16MB is all you will ever need. Period.

Windows 95's kernel footprint is roughly 4MB in real memory. That leaves a massive
12MB of unfragmented RAM for user applications. With 16MB installed, Quake runs
completely in cache, Netscape doesn't thrash, and Microsoft Office 95 loads in
under three seconds from a Western Digital Caviar drive.

Bill Gates took heat for saying 640K was enough, but in 1996, 16MB is truly the
plateau of diminishing returns. Anything over 16MB is pure corporate snake oil
promoted by DRAM manufacturers to artificially inflate 72-pin SIMM pricing.
Unless you are running a CAD workstation rendering Pixar animations in LightWave,
spending money on 32MB is throwing cash into a black hole.

Save the money and buy an S3 ViRGE 3D accelerator instead.
