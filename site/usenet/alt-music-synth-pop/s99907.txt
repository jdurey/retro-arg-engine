Hi everyone on alt.music.synth-pop:

Ever since the rumors began about the 1989 Orion Records destruction order,
I have been trying to track down one of the surviving European test pressings
of the Nova Hart album. Last week, I managed to acquire a white-label 12-inch
(plain black sleeve, no printed artist name, catalog stamp: OR-SYN-85).

Yesterday, I took the disc to our university physics department to look at
the runout groove under an Olympus stereo zoom microscope at 80x magnification.

Here is what is plainly visible in the vinyl:

1. The silent lead-out spiral between the last song and the locked groove
   is NOT silent. Instead of a smooth mechanical cut, the spiral exhibits
   a regular, undulating lateral wave with a period that corresponds to
   precisely 14.2 Hz at 33 1/3 RPM.

2. At intervals of approximately 1.4 seconds along the spiral, the wave
   inverts polarity by 180 degrees. This is classic binary phase-shift keying!

3. Hand-etched between the label rim and the runout groove is the signature:
     "B2-EXEC-1989 / M. HALE / THE SIGNAL IS OLDER"

4. When played through a high-end moving coil cartridge (Denon DL-103) into
   a spectrum analyzer, a clean peak at 14.2001 Hz registers clearly, even
   when the tone arm is resting in what should be silent dead air.

Orion's executives didn't just press music onto these records. Marcus Hale
turned every single copy of this vinyl into a permanent analog ROM disc.
Even if every digital master is demagnetized, the physical vinyl holds the
code in its polyvinyl chloride grooves forever.

Has anyone else inspected their copies from the Tokyo or Frankfurt pressings?
