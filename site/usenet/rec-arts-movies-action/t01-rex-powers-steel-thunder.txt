I just managed to pick up a mint-condition Vanguard Video big box VHS of Carl
Mitchell's 1985 action masterpiece STEEL THUNDER starring Rex Powers.

Everyone talks about Commando and Rambo II, but the sheer visceral intensity
of Rex Powers preparing his compound bow and customized Chevy Blazer in the
abandoned Arizona smelter while Heartstrike's "Fire in Your Heart" thrashes in
the background is unmatched.

Did Rex Powers do his own motorcycle jump over the Union Pacific freight train
in the third act, or was that stunt coordinator Kevin Carter? The tracking
shot from the Bell UH-1 Huey helicopter looks totally real.

Also, whatever happened to Heartstrike? That song had enough energy to power
a small municipality.

In article <1996Sep16.211044.4091@cinema.usc.edu>, Gary Chen writes:
> Did Rex Powers do his own motorcycle jump over the Union Pacific freight train
> in the third act, or was that stunt coordinator Kevin Carter?

Rex did 85% of his own stunts on Steel Thunder, but the train jump was performed
by veteran stunt driver Ray Spencer! Ray broke two ribs when the rear wheel clipped
the gravel embankment on the far side of the tracks, but they kept the take in
the final theatrical cut.

As for Heartstrike: they were a Cleveland rock band that moved to Malibu in 1985
right after Steel Thunder came out. Tragically, their singer Steve Vanderstok and
guitarist TJ Chicon died in a Ferrari Testarossa crash on Sunset Boulevard shortly
after leaving The Roxy.

They rebuilt the band with two Scandinavian brothers (Joss & Kent Freidnam) and
released an incredible progressive rock record called 'Lightning Strikes' in 1987.
Check out the fan tribute page on GeoCities at:
http://www.geocities.com/SunsetStrip/Club/1987/

The montage with Rex Powers still stands as one of the peak moments of 80s cinema.
