Got hold of a board from a surplus auction. Two MC68000P8s, a TMS32010,
64K of dual-ported SRAM and a PAL16R4 doing bus arbitration. Silkscreen
reads 'SD TWIN-68 REV C 1984 -- M.H.'. Reverse-engineered schematic is on
ftp.ashford-poly.edu in /pub/telecom-archives/stardust/twin68_array.asc

The odd part: the second 68000 never touches the DSP. It only drives a
14.2 Hz reference oscillator through a 12-bit DAC. Why would you burn a
whole CPU on that? One of the two EPROM sockets is empty.

Because you want the phase reference to be a deterministic function of
a key, not a free-running crystal. CPU B runs a keystream; CPU A
modulates against it. Without CPU B's ROM you can demodulate the
carrier, but nodes keyed that way come out as noise.

I designed arbitration PALs like that for a defense contractor in '83.
I'd recognise that layout anywhere.
