The 16MB RAM Manifesto: Why Anything Over 16 Megabytes Is Pure Marketing

At $34 per megabyte for 72-pin 60ns EDO SIMMs, a pair of 8MB sticks costs $544. Yet computer-superstore salesmen are telling college freshmen they need 32MB just to write term papers! Let's look at the engineering facts: first, half of the Socket 3 and early Socket 5 motherboards on the market (especially budget SIS and Opti chipsets with 128KB of L2 SRAM) cannot even cache memory above 16MB or 32MB without a second tag-RAM chip—meaning adding a third SIMM actually slows down your system!

Second, with a clean `CONFIG.SYS` loading `HIMEM.SYS` and `EMM386.EXE NOEMS` (giving you 621KB of free conventional memory), or under OS/2 Warp 3 and Linux kernel 1.2.13, 16MB leaves 11MB of untouched RAM disk even while compiling a 16,384-point FFT in C. Write tighter code instead of buying more silicon.

Overclocking the AMD 5x86-P75 (133 MHz) on a 40 MHz VESA Local Bus

If you set your Biostar MB-8433UUD bus clock jumpers (`JP14-JP16`) from 33 MHz to 40 MHz with a 4x multiplier (160 MHz core!), remember to add **1 wait state** to your VLB Tseng Labs ET4000/W32p video card (`JP1 CLOSED`). At 0 wait states on a 40 MHz bus, the pixel clock scrambles hardware cursors in XFree86 3.1.2.
