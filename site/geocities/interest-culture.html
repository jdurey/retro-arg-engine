In Defense of the Walkman WM-DD9 and the 90-Minute Solitude

There is a peculiar rhythm to walking through the rain in the 13th arrondissement with a quartz-locked Sony WM-DD9 clipped to your coat pocket. Unlike a portable CD player—which skips if you step off a curb too briskly—a well-calibrated amorphous-head cassette player turns the city into a continuous tracking shot. When Side A clicks off after 45 minutes, the mechanical silence reminds you to look up at the streetlamps.

From 'Bande FM Libre' (1981) to the 1995 Micro-Web

Many of the people building personal homepages in Paris/LeftBank today cut their teeth in the early 1980s soldering 15-watt FM transmitters in attic bedrooms for France's free-radio explosion (Radio Ivre, Fréquence Gaie, Radio Carbone 14). Commercial consolidation swallowed the FM dial by 1990, but HTML tables and cheap dial-up shells have reopened the attic studio.
