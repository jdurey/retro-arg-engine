E-mail takes four seconds to cross the ocean, which is wonderful—and also why it feels weightless. The Global C-90 & Postcard Registry pairs people in different time zones who still love pulling a battered airmail padded envelope out of the postbox containing a handwritten letter, two local tram tickets, and a 90-minute cassette recording of what their town actually sounds like.

Why We Record the Weather Forecast and the Ferry Horn (Anemoia in Practice)

Last month I received a Maxell XLII-90 in the post from a tram driver in Gothenburg, Sweden. Side B was thirty minutes of his cassette recorder sitting on the dashboard of Tram #7 during an October sleet storm: the rhythmic *clack-clack* of the relay contactors, the pneumatic hiss of the folding doors, and passengers shaking out wet umbrellas. Listening to it on headphones in sunny Crete gave me homesickness for a rainy Swedish street corner I have never stood on in my life.

How to Use International Reply Coupons (IRCs) & Avoid Customs Tape X-Rays

When mailing cassettes across borders, always write `MAGNETIC AUDIO TAPE — DO NOT X-RAY // NO COMMERCIAL VALUE` in thick black marker on both sides of the Jiffy bag, and wrap the cassette shell in a layer of kitchen aluminum foil just in case.
