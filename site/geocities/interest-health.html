*** CHECK YOUR MONITOR VERTICAL REFRESH RATE NOW: 60 HZ INTERLACED SVGA IS AN EYE-STRAIN HAZARD! ***

As millions of office workers and students move from paper desks to spending eight hours a day in front of 14-inch Cathode Ray Tubes (VDTs), occupational clinics are seeing an explosion of Repetitive Strain Injury (RSI) and 60 Hz flicker headaches. Here is evidence-based ergonomics for keyboard operators, audio engineers, and night-shift sysadmins.

Why Your 60 Hz Monitor Fights Your Office Fluorescent Ballasts

In North America, magnetic fluorescent ceiling ballasts flicker at 120 Hz (twice the 60 Hz mains frequency); in Europe, they flicker at 100 Hz. When your Windows 95 video driver defaults your S3 Trio64 card to `800x600 @ 60 Hz` (or worse, `1024x768 @ 87 Hz Interlaced`), the phosphor decay beats against the room lighting. Switching to **72 Hz or 75 Hz non-interlaced** reduces subconscious pupillary oscillation by over 60%.

Hearing Conservation for Headphone Engineers: The 85 dBA SPL Rule

Closed-back studio headphones like the Sony MDR-V6 have a razor-sharp 3 kHz presence peak designed to reveal tape hiss. When monitoring shortwave static or mastering tracks late at night, auditory fatigue tricks you into creeping the headphone potentiometer up 6 dB every hour. Calibrate a known 80 dB SPL pink-noise mark on your mixer and put a piece of white gaffer tape next to the knob.
