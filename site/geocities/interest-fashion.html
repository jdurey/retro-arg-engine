Forget department-store runway syndication. This index documents what people are actually wearing in the back-alleys of Jingumae (Ura-Harajuku), Broadway thrift bins in SoHo, and Manchester record fairs in October 1995: technical fleece, deadstock 1970s suede Pumas, and brushed-aluminum personal electronics worn as tailoring.

Hardware as Wardrobe: The Brushed-Titanium & Translucent Polycarbonate Shift

Look at the industrial design coming out of Tokyo this autumn: the Nikon 28Ti with its analog chronograph dials on top of a black titanium body, the Sony MZ-E3 MiniDisc walkman in champagne anodized aluminum, and translucent 'skeleton' Casio G-Shock DW-6900s. Clothing is following hardware: charcoal melton wool coats paired with reflective 3M Scotchlite piping and Velcro courier straps.

The Ura-Harajuku Small-Batch Print Ethos (Nowhere / Propeller Backstreets)

While big Shibuya department stores discount leftover 1991 Italian suits, tiny 12-square-meter shops down Cat Street are numbering heavyweight loopwheel T-shirts by hand from 1 to 100 and packaging them in heat-sealed anti-static electronics bags.
