*** WELCOME TO THE OUT-IN-CYBERSPACE WEBRING (STATION #14) // PGP 2.6.2 KEYRING UPDATED OCT 1995 ***

Since 1986, long before commercial ISPs existed, queer sysops kept community switchboards alive over late-night FidoNet echoes (`BMUG`, `SOC.MOTSS`, `GLBT-NET`) and independent photocopy zines. This homestead collects safe-harbor dial-up directories, independent 16mm festival calendars, and Hi-NRG / synth-pop 12-inch vinyl preservation lists.

How Rural Dial-Up & Usenet Changed Everything (1985–1995)

If you live in San Francisco, New York, London, or Amsterdam, you can walk down the street to an independent bookshop like *A Different Light* or pick up a free weekly paper. If you are nineteen years old in a town of 4,000 people with a shared family phone line and a 2400-baud modem, finding `soc.motss` or a GeoCities homepage at 1:00 AM is the first time you realize there is an entire world waiting for you.

The 128-BPM Vault: Preserving 1983–1989 Hi-NRG & Italo-Synth 12-Inch Dubs

Major labels are letting the greatest 12-inch club mixes of the 1980s rot in climate-uncontrolled warehouses because they only want to reissue 3-minute radio edits on CD compilations. Our tape-swap circle transfers original Patrick Cowley, Sylvester, Bronski Beat, and rare 1985 Japanese synth-pop promos at 15 IPS onto DAT and Type IV Metal cassettes.
