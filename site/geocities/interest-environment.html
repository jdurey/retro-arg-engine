Why 1200-Baud VHF Packet Radio Beats Satellite Uplinks in the Cloud Forest

University grant committees love buying $12,000 commercial satellite phones that die the first time epiphytic moss grows over the dish feedhorn. For $280 per tree, we bolt an Icom IC-2AT handheld, a PacComm Tiny-2 TNC, and a 20-watt Siemens solar panel into an ammo can 200 feet up in the canopy. Every four hours, the logger bursts 400 bytes of microclimate CSV data down to the valley university gateway (`igc.apc.org`).

Good News From the Stratosphere: NOAA Halocarbon Trends in 1995

For the first time since measurements began at Mauna Loa and Barrow, atmospheric growth rates of trichlorofluoromethane (CFC-11) and dichlorodifluoromethane (CFC-12) have flattened out following the Montreal Protocol phase-out. The Antarctic spring ozone hole is still deep this October, but the chemistry proves that international treaties backed by hard atmospheric spectroscopy actually work.
