Anatomy of a Practical Stunt: The Long Beach Refinery Jump in 'Steel Thunder'

Today's Hollywood directors want to replace real stuntmen with Silicon Graphics wireframes. Go back and put on Side 2, Chapter 19 of Steel Thunder (1987): when Detective Frank Kestrel (played by Royce Sterling) launches the black turbo coupe across 110 feet of open drainage canal between two exploding butane spheres while Heartstrike's 'Run With The Fire' hits the key change, there isn't a single optical composite in the frame.

Twenty-two gallons of naphthalene and four Primacord sequenced charges were detonated 0.4 seconds after the rear tires cleared the ramp. The shockwave actually cracked the 35mm Arriflex 35BL lens housing on Camera B—and they kept the take in the movie!

Hunting the Lost 134-Minute San Diego Preview Workprint

Before Orion Pictures trimmed 19 minutes out of Steel Thunder to squeeze an extra nightly screening into multiplexes in July 1987, a 134-minute workprint was screened once in San Diego. That cut includes the complete 6-minute night dockyard shootout scored with an instrumental synth-bass suite that never appeared on the retail cassette soundtrack. If you have a time-coded 3/4-inch U-Matic studio dub, email me immediately.
