The 1995 Sun-Dried Tomato & Espresso Bar Invasion: A Field Report

Five years ago, if you ordered a cup of coffee in Seattle or Minneapolis, a waitress named Dot poured it out of an orange-lip Bunn-O-Matic decanter for sixty-five cents with free refills. Today, every dry-cleaner on the block has been converted into a fern-filled espresso lounge serving $2.75 double tall hazelnut lattes and focaccia sandwiches buried under half a jar of oil-packed sun-dried tomatoes.

Don't get me wrong—I love a dark-roast pull from a manual La Pavoni lever machine—but we maintain this directory to protect the endangered 24-hour Formica counter where the hashbrowns are griddled under a bacon press.
