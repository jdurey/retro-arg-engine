How 'Heartstrike' Built the Wall of Sound on 'Chrome Velocity' (1985) and 'Steel Thunder' (1987)

People who only heard Heartstrike on compressed AM car radios think they were just another Sunset Strip arena band. Put on an original 1985 Orion first pressing of Chrome Velocity or the 1987 Steel Thunder 12-inch promo single of 'Run With The Fire': keyboardist Kenji Voss triggered a Yamaha TX816 rack (eight individual DX7 modules!) panned hard left/right into a pair of Roland Dimension D SDD-320 chorus units, while the kick drum keyed an analog noise gate on the bass synth.

The 1990 Corporate Purge: Why We Still Boycott 'Glass Hours'

By early 1990, Orion Records' new executive suite decided that real studio engineers, expensive 2-inch magnetic tape, and unpredictable artists were bad for quarterly margins. They dropped half the Sunset Strip rock roster, shuttered Sublevel B2, and spent $2 million promoting Luna Gray's Glass Hours (released April 12, 1990)—an album so scrubbed of sub-bass dynamics and human tape bleed it sounds like an elevator demo ROM.
