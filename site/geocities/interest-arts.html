Welcome to the GeoCities Arts & Independent Print Commons. You won't find glossy museum gift-shop JPEGs here—most of us are scanning halftone collages on flatbed UMAX scanners at 150 DPI so your 14.4k modem doesn't choke. This hub connects photocopy zinesters, rubber-stamp mail artists, Deluxe Paint IV pixel-pushers, and 16mm scratch-film projectionists across SoHo, Paris, and Vienna.

What's on the Zine Rack — Autumn 1995 Reviews

TONER & BONE #7 (Chicago, 28 pp., digest size, $1.50 or trade): Hand-typed on an IBM Selectric II with a script ball. Features a four-page interview with a projectionist who ran 35mm midnight reels of Steel Thunder and Czech stop-motion shorts at the Biograph until the platter motor caught fire.

MERCER STREET HALFTONE #12 (SoHo/NYC, 16 pp., $1 + two 32¢ stamps): Our house zine. Includes a comparative teardown of Letraset dry-transfer sheets vs. Aldus PageMaker 5.0 PostScript halftones, plus photographs of abandoned pneumatic mail tubes under Lower Manhattan.

LE PETIT FORMAT #4 (Lyon/Paris, bilingual French/English, 10 FF or IRC): Dedicated to disposable camera exposures taken from night trains between Gare de Lyon and Geneva.

The Great Digital Gradient Debate: Letraset vs. Kai's Power Tools

Every week someone posts to alt.zines asking whether they should ditch their X-Acto knives and wax-coaters for CorelDRAW 5 and Kai's Power Tools texture generators. Here is our manifesto: a neon rainbow fractal sphere on a laser-printed cover does not make a magazine. When you photocopy a photograph four generations down on 20-pound canary bond, the grain starts telling the truth. Keep your wax rollers warm.
